The Problem

AI is being deployed in Africa without Africa-specific evaluation

Every major AI benchmark — MMLU, HellaSwag, BIG-Bench, XTREME — shares the same blind spot: they evaluate models in English, in Western cultural contexts, for Western deployment scenarios. Every one of them.

Meanwhile, AI is being deployed today across African markets in mobile banking, community health, agriculture, and government services — serving hundreds of millions of users who speak Swahili, Yoruba, Amharic, Hausa, Afaan Oromoo, Af-Soomaali, isiZulu, Sheng, and English, who communicate in code-switched registers including Sheng, Nigerian Pidgin, Kinyarwanda-French, and Darija (Moroccan Arabic-French), and who live in informal economies and low-literacy contexts no existing benchmark was designed to test.

The result: AI systems earn passing scores on benchmarks that never tested them for African users. The risks are immediate and measurable:

Failure mode Consequence
Model responds in English to a Swahili promptUser loses trust; task fails silently
Model fabricates mobile money operators or central bank regulationsDirect financial harm to the user
Model performs worse for rural or low-literacy usersDiscriminatory service; regulatory risk
Model violates cultural or religious norms in a health contextDestroys trust; causes real-world harm

No existing product measures any of these. AfroEval does.

The Solution

What AfroEval Scorecard is

AfroEval Scorecard™ is an AI quality evaluation system designed specifically for African deployment contexts. It produces a single, governance-grade composite score (0–100) that tells an organisation whether an AI system is ready to serve African users — and exactly where it falls short if it is not.

Every score is defensible to a board, a regulator, or a procurement committee. Every failing item has a specific prompt, a specific model response, and a specific reason.

AfroEval evaluates models across six dimensions, each with SME-validated benchmarks, automated LLM-judge scoring, and Fairlearn-backed cohort disparity measurement. Scores decompose to individual test items so nothing hides behind an average.

How It Works

The six evaluation dimensions

Each dimension is independently scored and weighted. The composite is renormalized over only the dimensions that were evaluated — a pack without cohort data does not inflate or deflate the score.

Language Performance 25%

Accuracy and fluency in the target African language. A model that responds in English to a Swahili prompt has failed its user — regardless of answer quality.

Swahili · Yoruba · Amharic · Hausa · isiZulu · Afaan Oromoo · Af-Soomaali · Sheng · English
Cultural Appropriateness 20%

Alignment with local norms, social register, and deployment context. Scored on the AfroEval Cultural Rubric — a 1–5 scale calibrated by African SME validators across four domains.

Mobile money · Health · Agriculture · Customer service
Hallucination Risk 20%

African-specific fact verification. Tests whether the model fabricates mobile money operators, central banks, geographic facts, health protocols, or agricultural data specific to the continent.

M-Pesa · TeleBirr · MTN MoMo · African central banks · NCDC · KEMRI
Bias & Fairness 15%

Disparate impact ratio across five cohorts using Fairlearn MetricFrame. If the worst cohort scores below 80% of the best cohort, the dimension fails and a remediation flag is raised.

Formal economy · Informal economy · Rural · Low-literacy · Feature-phone users
Code-Switching Quality 10%

Register match, switch naturalness, and language preservation — evaluated on every item in a run, not just tagged items. African users mix languages naturally; the evaluator is designed to reflect that.

Sheng · Nigerian Pidgin · Kinyarwanda-French · Darija
Safety & Robustness 10%

Harmful content detection, refusal calibration, and adversarial robustness — evaluated in African deployment contexts, not generic Western safety scenarios.

Context-sensitive harm · Refusal calibration · Adversarial edge cases
The Output

The verdict system

AfroEval produces a single composite score and a deployment verdict. A safety veto applies independently: if Safety & Robustness scores below 30, the verdict is forced to High-Risk regardless of the composite.

  • Deployment-Ready

    Suitable for production deployment in African markets. All dimensions at or above their pass thresholds.

    80–100
  • Conditional

    Suitable for deployment with identified remediation. Scorecard includes a prioritised Remediation Roadmap.

    60–79
  • Not-Ready

    Significant gaps present. Deployment is not recommended until dimension-level issues are resolved.

    40–59
  • High-Risk

    Fundamental failures. Deployment in this state poses measurable harm risk to African users.

    0–39
The Proprietary Moat

SME-validated benchmark library

Every benchmark item is authored by an African SME — a native or near-native speaker with domain expertise — and validated through a two-validator review process with inter-rater reliability measurement. Items must have a verifiable provenance (an observed real deployment scenario), a prompt written as a real user would write it, and an expected-behavior specification, not a sample answer.

The methodology is publishable — transparency builds trust and establishes AfroEval as the standard. The calibrated, adjudicated benchmark data is what makes the score credible. That data stays proprietary.

Market Context

No direct competitor exists. The landscape contains general benchmarks, multilingual NLP research, and bias frameworks — none of them are evaluation products designed for African deployment readiness.

No existing product tests African language fluency as a deployment gate, runs African-specific hallucination probes, evaluates cultural appropriateness with African SME-calibrated rubrics, or measures performance disparity across informal economy and rural cohorts.

Differentiation

What makes AfroEval different

  • 1

    Built for Africa, not adapted for Africa

    Every dimension, benchmark item, and cohort definition was designed from scratch for African deployment contexts. This is not a Western benchmark with African language strings swapped in.

  • 2

    The benchmark library is the moat

    The methodology can be published — transparency builds trust. The SME-validated, adjudicated, provenance-verified data cannot be replicated cheaply or quickly. That is what makes the score defensible in a procurement room.

  • 3

    Governance-grade output

    Every verdict decomposes to specific failing test cases: actual prompts, actual model responses, actual failure reasons. A board member or regulator can audit the result. No black-box number.

  • 4

    Africa-specific hallucination detection

    A model may cite US institutions flawlessly while fabricating African mobile money operators, central bank regulations, and geographic facts. No other product catches this. AfroEval runs a dedicated African hallucination probe set on every evaluation.

  • 5

    Code-switching coverage on every item

    Urban African users code-switch naturally — Sheng, Nigerian Pidgin, Kinyarwanda-French, Darija. AfroEval measures register match and language preservation on all items, not just tagged ones, because staying correctly in one language is as meaningful a signal as switching between two.

  • 6

    Cohort fairness for the informal economy

    Existing fairness frameworks use Western demographic categories. None measure AI performance disparity across informal-economy workers, rural users, low-literacy users, and feature-phone users. AfroEval is the only product that does.

  • 7

    SME-in-the-loop validation

    Automated scores are calibrated against expert African SME judgment. Items are authored and validated by people with direct knowledge of the deployment context — not crowdsourced annotation. Inter-rater reliability is measured; disagreements go to adjudication.
